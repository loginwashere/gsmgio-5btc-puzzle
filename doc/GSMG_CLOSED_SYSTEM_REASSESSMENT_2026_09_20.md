# Closed-system reassessment — 2026-09-20

No solution was found. Two executed checks sharpen where to look next.

## What is worth keeping

The prime-position `b`/`be` grammar, original image endpoints, FEFE marker, and
23/16/7 profile are mutually comparable concrete objects. Phase 525's Selection A
adds a particularly compact interpretation: one representative per source byte
1–23, with FEFE replacing byte 21's ordinary endpoint. This is mechanically
verified but not uniquely creator-selected.

The unproved steps are different: interpreting DBBI as a checkerboard, assuming
it supplies FAED's board, forcing a mirror reconciliation, choosing a matrix
layout/value convention, and reading intermediate outputs as passwords. Do not
let those assumptions enter the next experiment as established facts.

   DBBI's checkerboard evidence is weaker than its current object page suggests.
   Twenty-three of 25 `b`s are already explained by its prime hosts. Among
   scaffold-preserving controls, `{b,e}` ranks uniquely first about 18% of the
   time; the observed best-of-36 IC closeness has conditional tails about
   0.055–0.065. This weakens one basis for requiring a mirrored FAED pair; that
   step is not established as required. It neither disproves a checkerboard nor
   refutes the separate Architect `BUT/HYE` observation.
2. [Phase 535](GSMG_PHASE535_SELECTION_A_MATRIX_COMPOSITION.md) checks a real
   omitted composition: new Selection-A endpoints through the old matrix rule.
   It removes the historical collisions but yields `IZLKESKGGIPPEN`, with final
   `e` still unplaced. Direct backward word indexing by its row sums also
   supplies no clear instruction. Two of five mappings are collision-free;
   within the two 84-token mappings A alone is collision-free. The 23/16/7
   counts alone permit 36 of 300 omissions. A also loses the old `SEED`
   substring, which was never independently validated. The improvement is
   geometric and conditional, not a net demonstrated gain in decoding.

## A way to search for the missing clue

Use **competing construction models with predictions**, rather than requiring
the entire unknown decoding recipe to be supplied by an explicit hint before
allowing any investigation. The six-field eligibility audit in Phase 531 tells
us no *fully bound* decoder is present in its inventory. It does not establish
that small exploratory inference is exhausted. Keep that distinction explicit.

These are research directions, not yet a closed executable experiment:

| Model | Already explained | Still has to explain |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-stream checkerboard | Candidate pair's IC, subject to Phase 534's confound | Actual readable output or an independently checkable consumer |
| Prime markers inserted around payload | Exact prime hosts and 23/16/7 split | Non-host symbols, their order, final `e`, and why these image endpoints |
| Spatial data addressed by the image | Collision-free Selection-A placement of hosted tokens | Numeric values, tail placement, and a complete use of `matrixsumlist` |

A candidate unit of investigation is the **non-host information**:
60 hosted payload symbols plus a terminal `e` in the 84-token parse, with group
boundaries preserved. Direct password use of its residue and rails is already
negative (Phase 530). That does not test whether those values describe a matrix
or encode instructions.

A concrete follow-up comparison was marker-only versus payload-only matrix sums at
the now-fixed Selection-A positions. The creator's existing “some characters
need to be zeroed out” clue motivates asking whether the `b`/`be` markers are
addresses that should not contribute to sums. It does **not** select that
interpretation. We preserved positions when zeroing; deleting raw prime positions
or checkerboard tokens, as in Phase 45, tests a different construction. The
existing all-token sums remained the control, with the unresolved `e` exposed
and the same output/consumer convention fixed before new results were viewed.
This follow-up is now included in Phase 535: payload-only output
`HVJIDTJDCGQNDJ`, and neither partition's backward word indices yields a clear
instruction. Thus that precise missing composition is now checked, not left as
an unrun recommendation.

The next useful *question* is whether the non-host data is a numeric/spatial
description at all, or whether the full stream has another encoding. Resolve
that by comparing explicit small generators that account for **all** symbols,
including the terminal `e`, and their predictions for another existing object.
No particular generator has yet earned priority as the intended decoder.

For any such exploratory family:

- State all choices and retain every tested member; do not silently repair an
  almost-word, change the alphabet, or move the tail after seeing output.
- Compare against controls preserving the prime hosts and payload histogram.
  Require the proposed method to recover planted instances before treating a
  miss as rejection of a cipher family.
- Prefer predictions about another existing artifact, complete structure, or
  an exact cryptographic target to a short English substring. A matching known
  address is powerful validation even when the decoder began as a hypothesis;
  an extra explicit creator instruction is not required to acknowledge that
  result. Padding or a suggestive substring alone is insufficient.
- Record which *composition* was tested. “We tested primes” or “we tested
  matrices” cannot close a later experiment whose tokenization or coordinates
  changed.

This program can use only the existing corpus. It makes progress by explaining
unconsumed data and correcting assumptions, without waiting for new hints or
repeating large password lists.

Review follow-up: the next executable family must separately freeze its finite
generators, input corpus, exact acceptance test, planted controls, and stop rule.
The direction above alone supplies none of those bindings. The 60-symbol
residue has chi-square 12.3 on 8 degrees of freedom (approximate p=0.138), which
fails to reject uniformity without establishing randomness. Its maximum
representational capacity is 190.20 bits, not measured entropy; this does not
exclude a seed or password that derives a 256-bit key.

**Executed follow-up:** [Phase 536](GSMG_PHASE536_PRIME_SCAFFOLD_NUMERIC_TEXT.md)
now supplies one such closed test: three numeric encodings of local text,
arbitrary fixed digit relabeling, and exact prime-host reinsertion. All seven
eligible planted cells recovered; all 407,532 corpus windows were checked;
zero full matches. This rejects that specific family and reaches its stop rule.
It does not elevate the broader model directions above to selected decoders.
