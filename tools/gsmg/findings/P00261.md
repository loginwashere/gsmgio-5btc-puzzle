## Phase 261 -- Phase 260 correction: the yin-yang drop cap is a book-wide design, not unique to the title's C/D (2026-08-13)

A fuller review of the book's photographed pages (the 73 2026-07-12 photos
plus the Phase 259 pages 57-58) found the same gold/black yin-yang drop-cap
design -- gold upper-left, black lower-right, with inverse-color dots --
reused on ordinary body-paragraph opening capitals throughout the book, not
applied only to the title's `C`/`D`.

**Independently re-verified, not taken on report.** Sampled several
subsection openings directly: the essay's very first paragraph (p.6, "In the
beginning...") uses a plain white/gray "I," no gold -- so the treatment is
not universal on every capital. But p.26's "One ancient creed..." opening
uses an "O" with the identical gold/black split and inverse dots, pixel-
confirmed at 1,397 gold pixels with the same detection threshold used for
the title's C/D (sha256
`5c22f796a470d23ec1532393d6d3a6c5644b90fcd750998329c7c31f3a74f030`). Pages 42
and 58's subsection
openings ("In the human psyche...", "Peppered across Europe...") are plain,
undecorated -- consistent with the design being reserved for specific
structural positions (roughly one per chapter subsection) rather than every
paragraph. `cosmic_duality_title_initials_yinyang_audit.py` now encodes the
page-26 cross-check as a permanent, pixel-verified regression rather than a
one-off visual claim.

The exact total count of book-wide instances (a report puts it at 39, one
per subsection across the four chapters, with per-chapter first-letter
sequences tested for reversed/odd-even/column/concatenated readings, all
negative) was not independently re-tallied here -- the sampling above is
sufficient to confirm the *design is reused*, which is the fact that changes
Phase 260's conclusion; re-deriving an exact count of 39 across all 73
photos was not necessary to correct the overclaim and was not attempted.

**What changes and what doesn't.** Still true: the yin-yang glyph styling
itself is real and pixel-confirmed; it is completely absent from the front
cover's plain-silver rendering of the same title (a different, still-valid
data point -- cover vs. interior-page treatment differs regardless of this
correction); `CD` read as Roman numerals is still exactly `400`, unforced.
What changes: Phase 260 argued the title-page-only decoration meant the
typography "independently selects" `C`/`D` for a Roman reading, upgrading
`400` to something "independently echoed by an authenticated physical
artifact." Since the same decoration is a book-wide house style that would
land on whatever two letters happen to open the title (here, coincidentally,
`C` and `D`), it does not independently select those two letters at all --
the match to `400` is better graded an interesting possible coincidence or
corroboration, not an independent echo. Recovered pages 57-58 (Phase 259)
supply nothing further either way: neither page opens a new subsection, so
neither would be expected to carry a drop cap, and neither does.

The most useful further check, not performed here, would be comparing
against another *Mysteries of the Unknown* volume: identical yin-yang drop
caps in a different book would confirm series-wide publisher design
(closing this thread entirely); confined to this volume alone, the choice
remains thematically deliberate for a book about duality, but still not an
encoded orientation sequence.
