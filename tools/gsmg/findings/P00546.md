## Phase 546 — selectivity curve for the BTCSEED prefix over Bifid squares, cherry-pickable under the declared broad class (2026-09-21)

**Question:** Over a wide population of Bifid squares, how often does the community decode of FAED give a
prefix that could be read as meaningful? A calibration gate on the prefix as evidence; not a decoder.

**Frozen inputs:** FAED and decoder fixed; Arm A = all 9! squares with `A..I` in the first nine cells, then
`K..Z`; Arm B = 20,000,000 uniform random squares. Classes E1 (`BTCSEED`), E2 (7-letter dictionary word),
E3 (E2, or a 3-letter token plus a 4-letter dictionary word, either order; tokens include `BTC`). Gate on
Arm A's E3 count `k3` (>= 37 cherry-pickable, <= 3 selective) with declared `T_max` = 10,000. Lock pins
protocol, script, test, dictionary, class digests, seed and controls.

**Method:** Controls before the lock: vectorised prefix vs full decode, real ordering reproduces `BTCSEED`
and Phase 425's hash, class machinery, exact-vs-Monte-Carlo counting on non-vacuous toy classes.

**Result:** Arm A: E1 8, E2 2, E3 4,948 of 362,880 (E3 rate 1.36%). Arm B: E1 2, E2 33, E3 112,028 of 20M
(0.56%). Outcome `cherry_pickable`. The most frequent E3 prefixes are junk (`FAAIEEE`, `ALAIEEE`); the strict
classes are rare (E1 2.2e-5 in Arm A, 1e-7 in Arm B).

**Disposition:** calibration gate, not a lead. The prefix cannot separate design from post hoc reading under
a two-word reading; it does not say `BTCSEED` is accidental and leaves Phase 425's fixed-target p unchanged.

**Facts affected:** None.

**Supersedes/corrects:** None. Complements Phase 425 (fixed target) with a selection-side calibration.
