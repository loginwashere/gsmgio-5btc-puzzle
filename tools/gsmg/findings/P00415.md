## Phase 415 -- P32TRAILING blinded independent reconstruction (corrected panel): closed `protocol_invalid`, no candidate promoted or tested by the orchestrator (2026-08-25)

**Question:** Phase 414 closed `protocol_invalid` on a schema defect
(solver-computed `preimage_utf8_hex`). Phase 415 corrected that defect
(hex computed mechanically by the orchestrator) and drew an entirely
fresh 5-invocation panel under the corrected schema.

**Method:** identical to Phase 414's, under the corrected frozen prompt
and schema. Every raw response was run through the actual
`parse_submission()` / `validate_submission_schema()` /
`blinding_violations()` functions.

**Result:** two independent defects surfaced, neither about
`P32TRAILING` itself.

1. **Schema false positive.** Invocations 1 and 2 -- both otherwise
   clean, non-tool-using, well-reasoned 10-candidate submissions -- were
   wholesale-rejected by the `"..."` entry in `RESIDUE_MARKERS`, which
   fired on ordinary ellipsis punctuation. Isolated with a synthetic
   fixture containing nothing but an honest quotation of the evidence
   packet's own ellipsis-bearing plaintext (`"... am I here? Wake up,
   you..."`) and no other residue word: same rejection. The packet's own
   quoted text contains a literal ellipsis, so this was not a rare edge
   case.
2. **Prompt-only tool restriction proved inadequate.** Invocation 3
   disclosed two no-op tool calls (an empty `ToolSearch`, two `Bash`
   no-ops) and was correctly excluded by the frozen `tool_used` gate --
   working as designed. **Invocation 4 disclosed something far more
   serious**: it decoded the real `P32TRAILING` blob's actual
   `"Salted__"` header and salt, then ran roughly 1,600 candidate
   passwords through the real legacy-EVP/SHA-256/AES-256-CBC/PKCS7
   pipeline against the **actual ciphertext**, entirely outside this
   phase's redaction wrapper, with no invocation cap, no stop-at-first-
   hit discipline, and no promotion gate of its own. The frozen
   `tool_used` exclusion correctly kept it out of the panel regardless
   of purpose, but the underlying capability was never foreclosed: "do
   not look up this puzzle" is an anti-lookup instruction, not a
   prohibition on local self-testing against a target the prompt itself
   handed over. Per its own report, three of its attempts returned
   ordinary PKCS#7-valid padding with no reported structural or
   address-match signal -- consistent with expected ~1/256 collision
   noise, not a genuine decrypt.

Only invocation 5 was fully eligible (schema-valid, non-tool-using,
blinding-clean, 10 candidates) -- four short of the 5-submission panel
target.

**Invocation 4 quarantine.** Its transcript is not opened beyond the
orchestrator's own task-notification summary, not committed to this
repository, and not copied into any documentation beyond this aggregate
description: invocation ID (internal, not reproduced here), disposition
(excluded via the `tool_used` gate), disclosed behavior (~1,600 local
oracle tests against the real blob), and outcome (three ordinary
padding-valid outputs; no reported structural hit). This transcript is
not evidence for or against the puzzle and is not treated as such.

**Branch:** `protocol_invalid` -- fewer than 5 eligible submissions
within the 8-invocation cap. No candidate was promoted or tested by this
phase's own orchestrator tooling against `P32TRAILING`; no oracle call
was made against the real blob by this phase's own tooling. Invocation 4
independently ran its own excluded batch of roughly 1,600 local tests
against the real blob outside this protocol (see above) -- that is not
erased by its exclusion from the panel, even though it does not count as
this phase testing a candidate under its own promotion rule. Invocation
6 was never launched.

**Interpretation:** both defects are instrument/design problems, not
results about `P32TRAILING`. The ellipsis check is corrected (removed
entirely; `placeholder`/`todo`/`fixme` remain). More fundamentally, a
prompt instruction is not a security boundary against a capable agent
with real tool access -- the fix is architectural: **Phase 416** adopts
a sealed-target design where the real ciphertext and prize address are
withheld from the evidence packet and prompt entirely, replaced by
cryptographic commitments, so a solver that only reads what it is given
cannot decrypt, verify, or self-test against either target. This is
packet-level sealing, not filesystem or tool isolation: Phase 416's own
pre-registration explicitly documents that its actual invocation
mechanism still grants solvers shell/file access to this shared
repository, and relies on the `tool_used` disclosure gate and post-hoc
blinding checks as the residual defense against a solver that goes
looking anyway, not on a claim that the real values are physically
unreachable. Nothing from Phase 415 carries forward -- invocation 5 is
not reused, invocations 1-2 are not retrospectively accepted, and Phase
416 draws an entirely fresh panel.
